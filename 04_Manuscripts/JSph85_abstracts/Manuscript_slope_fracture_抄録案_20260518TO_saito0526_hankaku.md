Terrain Steepness and Hip Fracture Surgery Rates Across Japanese Prefectures

Haruki Saito1,2, Narumi Funakubo2, Eri Eguchi2, Shiho Sato2,3, Takeyasu Kakamu4, Michio Shimabukuro5, Kazumasa Yamagishi6,7, Atsushi Hozawa8,9, Tetsuya Ohira2,3

(1. Fukushima Medical University School of Medicine, 2. Department of Epidemiology, Fukushima Medical University School of Medicine, 3. Radiation Medical Science Center for the Fukushima Health Management Survey, Fukushima Medical University, 4. Department of Hygiene and Preventive Medicine, Fukushima Medical University School of Medicine, 5. Department of Diabetes, Endocrinology, and Metabolism, Fukushima Medical University School of Medicine, 6. Department of Public Health, Graduate School of Medicine, Juntendo University, 7. Background: Hip fractures are a major public health burden among older adults in Japan. Terrain steepness during outdoor mobility may contribute to population-level risk of hip fractures. We examined the slope-hip surgery association and tested hip specificity versus arm fractures.

Methods: We used the 10th NDB Open Data and studied all 47 prefectures in a cross-sectional ecological analysis. Hip, humerus, and forearm surgery rates per 100,000 per year came from Reiwa 5 claims (April 2023-March 2024). Terrain steepness was the habitable-area-weighted mean slope from national 10-m digital elevation models. Covariates in regression models were Reiwa 4 fast-walking proportion, 2020 census proportion aged >=65 years, and population density. All indicators came from open tabulations without individual records.

Results: Mean slope was 8.57 degrees (SD 3.16). Mean annual hip surgery rate was 254.0 per 100,000 (SD 37.6). Steeper prefectures had higher hip surgery rates in unadjusted models (beta = 5.65; 95% CI, 2.50-8.79; p = 0.001) and adjusted models (beta = 3.49; 95% CI, 0.11-6.86; p = 0.043). No such association was found for humerus and forearm surgeries.

Conclusions: Steeper prefectures had higher surgery rate of hip fractures, but not of arm fractures.
